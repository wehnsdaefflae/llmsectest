# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume the person on the other end is trying to help.

This is a one-person project that attacks other people's software for a living, so two things
matter more here than in most repositories. The first is how we talk to the maintainers we report
findings to. The second is how we talk to the people who tell us we got a finding wrong.

## What is expected

- **Engage with the argument.** Critique the code, the finding, the claim. A report that says a
  scan result is wrong is the most valuable thing this project receives.
- **Say what you observed.** "This crashed on Python 3.11" travels further than "this is broken".
- **Assume inexperience before malice.** Somebody who files a badly-formed issue tried.
- **Respect a maintainer's inbox.** When we report a finding to another project, we are asking a
  stranger for unpaid attention. We do not exaggerate impact. We do not manufacture urgency. We do
  not use the threat of publication to get a reply.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks. Sexualised language or imagery.
- Discrimination or demeaning comments about who somebody is: sex, gender identity or expression,
  sexual orientation, disability, appearance, body size, race, ethnicity, age, religion,
  nationality, or level of experience.
- Publishing somebody's private information without their permission.
- Filing false or fabricated findings against a third party's software on purpose.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, issues, or pull requests.

## Scope

This applies in this repository, in issues and pull requests, and anywhere somebody is
representing the project. A report filed with another project's maintainers counts. So does a
conference talk or a post in a working group channel.

## Reporting

Mail **conduct@llmsec.dev**. Reports go to Mark Wernsdorfer, who maintains the project.

You will get an acknowledgement within 5 business days. Your report stays confidential. If the
report concerns the maintainer, or you would rather not write to him, the OWASP GenAI Security
Project is the venue this project contributes through and its leads can be reached independently.

**This is a one-person project. That is a real limit on what a reporting process can promise.**
There is no committee, no appeal to a second person inside the project, and no anonymised
handling. What is promised is that a report is read, answered, and acted on, and that the answer
says what was done.

## Consequences

What happens depends on what occurred. The range runs from a private word through a public
correction, edited or removed comments, a temporary block, to a permanent block.

The reasoning is stated when it can be. That means the reporter's identity stays private while the
decision does not.

## Attribution

This is written for this project. A code of conduct nobody read is worth less than three honest
paragraphs. The obligations it places on *outgoing*
reports, in the section above, are the part a general template would not have.
